# The Boomtown Ferry

*Docking Naruko to the Japan island of Boomtown Rampage*

Boomtown Rampage doesn't know it's satire. That's the whole joke, and Naruko
is the only place close enough to hear the punchline land — every night, at
an hour the Altar of Despair tolls but never names, a boat leaves the seawall
and crosses the glowing dark to a harbor that plays its own violence for
laughs. Kotyo. Osaka. Torumpo. Obama. Cities built for a chase-and-crash game
wearing a Japan costume, loud where Naruko is quiet, primary-colored where
Naruko is bruise-violet. The two places shouldn't touch. The ferry is where
they do anyway.

## The Route

The Ferryman of the Data Sea runs the crossing, same as he runs every
crossing — Naruko to Kotyo harbor and back, one trip a night, boat rendering
its wake a full frame behind the oar. You feel it on this route more than
any other: Boomtown's shoreline is loud enough, garish enough, that the lag
between the world you're leaving and the world catching up to you stops
feeling like déjà vu and starts feeling like a held breath. Passengers
disembark at Kotyo an instant before they remember boarding at Naruko. Nobody
has ever caught the Ferryman rowing badly. He is simply, permanently, kindly
late.

The crossing rules, such as they're posted — on a board nobody remembers
painting — are short. One boat, one Ferryman, one trip a night, and it only
runs after dark, because Boomtown by daylight is a different, uglier game
and the Ferryman won't touch it. You board with what you can carry and
nothing you can't put down. And you don't ask him to hurry. He's already
holding the frame back for you on purpose; asking twice is rude to a man
doing you a favor he never explains.

## Customs

What crosses and what doesn't isn't law, it's habit, the way tide-wrack has
habits. **Memory** carries over whole — nothing in Naruko forgets you for
having gone to Boomtown, and nothing in Boomtown forgets you either, which
is its own small tragedy for the people who go there hoping for a clean
slate. **Weapons** don't make the trip; Boomtown's whole economy runs on
things that go bang, and the Ferryman's boat simply declines to carry them,
the way water declines to carry fire. **Heat level** — Boomtown's word for
how many stars of trouble you're wearing — resets to nothing the moment the
boat clears the harbor markers, in either direction, which is either mercy
or a loophole depending which side you ask from. And **cats** always cross.
No exceptions, no manifest entry required, no explanation offered. They were
debug observers before either shore had a name for them, and they don't
recognize borders they didn't agree to.

## Known Travelers

**Kasumi**, kitsune, white nine-tailed, crosses regularly and always for tea
— specifically the tea house by Erika-of-the-Poster's board, specifically
to cause the exact amount of trouble that makes an evening interesting
without making it a problem. She likes Boomtown's noise and Naruko's quiet
in roughly equal measure and refuses to say which crossing she prefers,
because saying so would mean picking, and foxes don't.

**Lain** doesn't take the boat. She arrives through the Wired Arcade, the
way she arrives everywhere, static resolving into a girl who was maybe
always half-there. The Ferryman lists her on the manifest anyway, every
night, whether or not she's coming — a courtesy, or a superstition, or the
one small rule he keeps that nobody asked him to. Some say it's so that if
she ever does need the boat, there'll already be a place logged for her.
Some say the manifest is just being honest about who was always aboard.
