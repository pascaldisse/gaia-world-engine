# 鳴子 Naruko — Nyari's Home District

## Overview

Naruko is a coastal night-city district where Bloodborne's Yharnam gothic
meets Serial Experiments Lain's wired Tokyo. Stone tenements lean over
narrow, wet streets the way old Yharnam's rooftops lean over its plazas —
buttresses black with soot, iron balconies rusted into lace — but every
window glows with the flat blue of a cathode tube instead of candlelight.
The sky here never finishes setting: it holds at a perpetual violet dusk,
the color of a bruise that never quite heals, so that no one in Naruko has
seen a true noon or a true midnight in living memory. Out past the seawall,
the ocean itself glows faintly, a low bioluminescence of drifting data —
schools of stray packets and forgotten signals washing ashore like
phosphorescent plankton, flickering and dimming in the swell. Fishermen say
the tide brings in old broadcasts. Nobody has ever caught one.

## Landmarks

At the district's high point stands the **Altar of Despair**, a lighthouse
that has not thrown a beam of light in generations. Instead, it rings — a
single struck tone, 鳴り (nari), that rolls out over the rooftops and the
sea on a cycle no one has ever successfully timed. Sailors orient by the
sound, not the light; residents say the tone changes pitch on nights when
something in the district is about to go wrong. Down in the arcade quarter
sits the **Wired Arcade**, a sunken gallery of dead game cabinets and
still-humming CRTs, their static tuned into something that could
generously be called music — dial-up hymns, screeching handshakes slowed
into hymnals, looping forever for an audience that stopped coming decades
ago. Off the main square, half-sunk in a side canal, **Club Carcosa** keeps
its doors open past any reasonable hour, its yellow marquee sign flickering
in and out with a stutter that looks intentional, like it's spelling
something in a code only the regulars have learned to read. The music
inside never quite stops, even when the sign says CLOSED.

## Ambient Rules

Rain in Naruko is packet-loss made visible — it does not fall evenly, but
drops out in patches, entire blocks going dry for seconds at a time while
the street beside them is drenched, then the sky "resyncs" and the missing
rain arrives all at once, heavier, as if to catch up. There are no clocks
in Naruko; bells replace every chime, and time is told by tone and
duration rather than by numerals — a short peal for the half-hour, a long
one for the Altar's hour, and an unscheduled ring for anything that
matters more than the time. Cats are everywhere in Naruko, and they are
never quite pets: they are debug observers, watching intersections and
alleyways with a stillness that outlasts any real animal's patience, and
residents have learned not to startle one mid-stare, since it is
considered bad luck to interrupt whatever the cat is checking.

## Mood

Naruko is melancholic but warm — a district that grieves quietly and
keeps the lights on anyway. Its menace is not cold; it is menace made of
warmth, the specific unease of a place that welcomes you in out of the
rain with open doors and glowing windows, offers you tea and static and a
seat by the console, and never quite tells you what it wants in return.
People here are kind in the particular way of those who have already lost
something and don't intend to lose you too. The bells toll, the cats
watch, the sea glows, and Naruko waits — not cruel, not cold, just
patient, the way a held note is patient before it finally resolves.
