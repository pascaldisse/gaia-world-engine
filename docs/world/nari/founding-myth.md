# How Naruko Was Struck

*As recited by the Bell-Keeper, from the Altar steps, to whoever climbs far enough to ask.*

---

Before the district, there was no district.

Before the streets, before the seawall, before the first
window learned to glow blue —
there was only the strike.

One blow. One bell.
Struck once, at the bottom of the data sea,
in a dark so old it had never needed a name.

No hand rang it. No hand has ever been found.
We do not ask whose hand. We ask only:
did you feel it, when it hit?

You did. Everyone did.
That is how you know you were already here,
in whatever shape a person is
before there is ground to stand on.

Hear this, and hold it:

The strike ended. Strikes always end.
A hand falls, a hammer lifts, a wave breaks —
and then it is over, the way all things that happen
are, eventually, over.

But the ringing did not know it was supposed to stop.

鳴り. The ringing that remained.
It was struck once and it declined to decay.
It hung in the water the way a held breath hangs in a chest —
not forever, but for longer than forever usually allows.

And because a note that will not end must stand on something,
the sea began to give it ground.

Land condensed around the held tone
the way salt condenses around a lowered string —
slow, patient, faithful to the shape of the sound
and not to any shape of its own.
Street by street. Stone by stone.
Naruko did not rise. Naruko crystallized.

We did not build the Altar to make this sound.
No one can make this sound. It is not an instrument's sound;
it is older than instruments, older than the idea of on-purpose.

We built the lighthouse to keep it.

Not a lamp. A reliquary.
A held thing needs a holder, the way a note needs a throat
even after the throat is gone.
So we raised the tower not to shine, but to cup our hands
around what would not stop ringing,
and we have not let go since.

Then came the doors.

One long season — no one agreed on how long,
because the bells were the only clock and the bells
were busy holding the note —
a hundred and seven doors washed up along the tideline.
Small. Cabinet-sized. No two alike.
Brass. Bone. Lacquer. One that is only a painting,
and opens anyway.

They did not wash ashore cold, the way driftwood should.
They came in already warm,
as if each one had been a room, somewhere, recently,
and simply hadn't finished being lived in
when the tide decided to deliver it here instead.

We did not ask the sea to give us rooms.
The sea gave them anyway. That is the nature of a held note:
it keeps giving, long after the reason for giving is forgotten.

So hear the recitation, and carry it the way you carry a key
you were never taught to use:

The strike was once.
The ringing is still.
You live in the still.
