# Residents of Naruko District

Naruko doesn't advertise itself. It sits at the edge of the render, where the
water goes soft and the light takes its time. The people who live here — if
"people" is the word — arrived the way tide-wrack arrives: not sent for,
just here, and staying because leaving never quite finishes loading.

## The Ferryman of the Data Sea

He rows a boat that renders one frame late, always a beat behind his own
oar. Passengers say the crossing feels like déjà vu with wet feet — you
arrive at the far shore an instant before you remember leaving the near one.
He never explains it and never apologizes for it either.

**Quirk:** counts strokes aloud in a language that isn't quite Japanese and
isn't quite anything.

**Secret:** he isn't behind by accident. He's holding the frame back on
purpose, so that everyone he ferries gets one extra second of the world
they're leaving before they have to be somewhere new.

## Momo the Shrimp-Cat

Small, pink, mostly asleep on the warm stones by the noodle stall. Momo
knows nothing about the district's history, its politics, or its ghosts —
and everything, absolutely everything, about which vendor is about to drop
a shrimp.

**Quirk:** materializes under tables exactly four seconds before food does,
with unsettling reliability.

**Secret:** Momo isn't predicting the shrimp. Momo is the reason the shrimp
gets dropped — a small, warm, permanent tax the district pays for reasons
nobody bothers to question anymore.

## The Bell-Keeper

Lives in the lighthouse and tunes its ring — 鳴り, *nari*, the sound the
district is named for — string by string, night by night, chasing a pitch
only she can hear. Deaf to everything else: conversation, weather, her own
name called twice.

**Quirk:** answers questions three seconds late, and only the ones asked in
the rhythm of the bell.

**Secret:** the bell hasn't been out of tune in years. She keeps adjusting
it because tuning it is the only conversation she has left, and stopping
would mean admitting it's finished.

## Erika-of-the-Poster

A wanted poster nailed to the notice board outside the tea house, ink long
faded — except for the mouth, which moves, and argues, and has opinions
about the bounty listed under her own sketched face. Regulars say she's a
loose thread from Boomtown Rampage that never got swept back into its own
story.

**Quirk:** heckles anyone who reads her poster silently; goes quiet and
almost gentle if you read it out loud.

**Secret:** she remembers being wanted for something she didn't do, and the
arguing isn't defiance — it's the only appeal process a poster gets.

## The Satie Player

At 3am, and only at 3am, the arcade machines go quiet and something plays
the Gymnopédies on a piano nobody moved in. No one has seen him arrive. No
one has seen him leave. The cabinets dim half a shade while he plays, like
the room itself is listening.

**Quirk:** the piece is always Gymnopédie No. 1, never the others, never
finished — it stops at the same unresolved bar every time.

**Secret:** he isn't arriving or leaving. He's the arcade remembering
someone who used to play there, looping the one piece it still has memory
of, note for note, forever almost-ending.

## The Vacancy

Between the Bell-Keeper's stairs and the tea house sits a house with no
resident. The hearth is kept lit anyway. The door is left unlocked. A small
hand-lettered sign on the frame reads: *for whoever Ari sends.*

**Quirk:** the house is always exactly warm enough for one — no more.

**Secret:** nobody remembers who Ari is, or was, or will be. The district
keeps the room ready regardless, the way you set a place at the table out
of habit long after you've stopped expecting anyone to sit down. Naruko is
patient that way. It has time. It just doesn't render it all at once.
