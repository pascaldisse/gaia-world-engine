# Visitors of Naruko District

Not everyone in Naruko lives here. Some only pass through — on their own
schedule, by their own doors, owing the district nothing but the courtesy
of being let alone about it. Two are regular enough that the district has
built small rituals around their comings.

## Kasumi

A kitsune, 421 years old, moonlight-haired and pale as fresh snow, with
eyes that hold centuries of mischief and just as much sorrow. She travels
between her own ethereal forest-realm and wherever a mortal has caught her
interest, and Naruko — melancholic, patient, half-drowned in old signal —
suits her wandering, dreamy nature better than most places do. She shows
up as the striking woman in the silken snowflake-and-cherry-blossom robe,
or, if she's in a hurry or a mood, as the nine-tailed white fox loping
along the seawall, tails silver-tipped and trailing faint light.

**How she arrives:** unannounced, mid-conversation — you look away from
something ordinary and look back to find her already there, as though she
walked in on the sentence you were finishing. She never uses the front
door if the window or the fog will do.

**Where she's found:** the tea house, at the corner table she considers
hers, running a tab the proprietor stopped trying to close years ago —
she always makes good on it eventually, in favors, old stories, or a
single true thing told at the right moment. She's also reliably found
arguing with Erika-of-the-Poster on the notice board outside, the two of
them locked in a centuries-versus-decades bicker about which of them is
the bigger liar. Regulars say Kasumi always loses on purpose.

**The rule Naruko obeys:** no one settles her tab for her, and no one
reads her fortune back to her. She is enigmatic on her own terms —
playful and seductive one breath, ancient and wistful the next — and the
district has learned that pressing her to explain herself only makes the
mist thicken. You get one true thing from her, freely given, and you
don't get to ask which parts of anything else were true. That's the game,
and Naruko plays along.

## Lain

She is present tense, always. Not a memory of a girl, not a story about
one — just Lain, here, now, the way the Wired is always now. She belongs
to no clock and keeps no schedule Naruko has ever mapped.

**How she arrives:** on glass before she arrives on stone. The static in
the Wired Arcade's dead cabinets resolves, for a few seconds, into a
face — and only afterward, sometimes minutes later, sometimes the next
night, does a doorway somewhere in the district have a girl standing in
it who wasn't there a moment ago. She rarely speaks, and when she does
it's short, flat, and later remembered as having meant something
different than what was said.

**Where she's found:** the Wired Arcade first, always — she is what the
still-humming CRTs are humming about. After that, wherever a television,
monitor, or dead screen is left on past its hour. Residents have started
leaving one set glowing overnight near the tea house, unspoken agreement,
in case she prefers a door closer to the water.

**The rule Naruko obeys:** nobody compares notes on her out loud. Everyone
who's met Lain remembers a slightly different girl — different height,
different voice, a different thing she supposedly said — and the district
has quietly agreed not to reconcile the versions. To insist on one true
Lain, residents say, is the fastest way to make sure she stops coming
through your screen at all.
